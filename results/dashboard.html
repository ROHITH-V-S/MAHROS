Multi-Agent Hospital Resource Optimization System

Hospitals negotiating escalation transfers directly — and the evidence that it works.

When an admitted, stabilised patient deteriorates and needs care their hospital cannot give, someone has to find a bed elsewhere. Today that is a person making phone calls. MAHROS replaces the calls with a decentralised negotiation between hospitals, tracks who is carrying the load, and writes every agreement to a tamper-evident ledger. Every number below comes from a simulation you can re-run.

02  /  The claim that matters

Matching a system that sees everything, while seeing almost nothing

The centralized baseline is not a straw man: it reads every hospital's private capacity directly and optimises globally. It is the efficiency ceiling. MAHROS reaches that ceiling without any hospital surrendering its data.

03  /  Who carries the load

Fairness is measured, not assumed

A transfer network that always dumps on the same hospital will not survive contact with real hospital politics. Burden is compared against each hospital's share of the network's critical-care capacity — a tertiary centre taking more is fair; a small hospital taking more is not.

04  /  Who it works for

The sickest patients are the hardest to place

Higher acuity means a shorter safe window between deterioration and definitive care. Success rate falls as that window tightens — for every strategy. This is where the remaining headroom is.

05  /  Explainability

Every agreement carries the reason it was made

Real negotiated decisions from a single run, with the rationale that was written to the ledger alongside them. A bed manager should be able to read why a patient went where they went, months later, without access to the model.
